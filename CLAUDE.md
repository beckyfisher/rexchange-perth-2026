## 1. What this project is

A **talk repository**, not an R package and not a research compendium. It contains the
abstract, slides, speaker notes and reproducible figure code for a conference
presentation.

**Event:** R Exchange Perth 2026, 14–15 October 2026, Perth CBD.
Organised by [Epi](https://epi.group) (B Corp, Posit Professional Services Partner).
**Day two, 2:15–2:30pm** (checked against the published schedule on 2026-10-06), between
Matt Fraser (Minderoo, 2:00pm) and François Michonneau (Posit, 2:30pm). Questions are taken at
a session Q&A at 3:00pm, so the slot is all talk time.

**Audience:** open-source data science professionals — R and Python users across
disciplines, interested in open science. Event themes include data visualisation, AI
integration, automated reporting, dashboarding, and open-source workflows in enterprise.
Assume they are technically strong and know nothing about ecotoxicology.

**Speaker:** Dr Rebecca Fisher, quantitative ecologist / ecological risk assessment
modeller, Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS).

**Talk length: 15 minutes.** The programme fixes it; earlier drafts were built for 20. On
`main` the timing markers in the notes add to about 14.7 minutes, and the words she reads (the
slide text she reads out, and the notes) to about 15.8 minutes at 140 words a minute (13.8 at
160). The revision of the AI section on 2026-10-13 added the `bayesnec` slide and nine
clicks, and left no time for explaining the figures; before it the counts were 12.5 and 13.1
minutes. On 2026-10-13 she said she is comfortable with that length, because she speaks
quickly. A slide added means time taken elsewhere.

**The abstract, title and bio are fixed.** They are published in the programme. Do not edit
the submitted text in `talk/abstract.qmd`; the deck has to deliver what it promises.

**Versions.** `main` is the version to be presented: AIMS template, 15 minutes,
chronological. The `discharge-first` branch is an earlier 20-minute cut that has not been
updated. The `pre-review-2026-10-06` branch is the AIMS-template deck as the speaker first
reviewed it; it was never committed at the time and was recovered on 2026-10-07 from Claude
Code's saved file copies. Do not delete any of them without asking.

**Do not conflate the two routes to a regulatory number.** The dredging thresholds
(Fisher et al. 2018, *J. Appl. Ecol.* 55:415) come from *in situ* dose–response plus ROC
curves and **predate** the SSD work; `ssdtools`/`bayesnec` are about SSD-derived national
guideline values. `notes/slide-outline.md` §"Routes to a regulatory number" sets them side by
side.

### Rendering

```powershell
& "C:\Program Files\R\R-4.5.1\bin\Rscript.exe" R/figures.R
& "C:\Program Files\R\R-4.5.1\bin\Rscript.exe" R/aims-assets.R
& "C:\Program Files\R\R-4.5.1\bin\Rscript.exe" R/local-images.R
& "C:\Program Files\R\R-4.5.1\bin\Rscript.exe" R/build-speaker-notes.R
& "C:\Program Files\Positron\resources\app\quarto\bin\quarto.exe" render
```

`quarto render` builds the reveal.js deck and the abstract. `R/export-deck.R` (run in WSL R,
which has `officer`) then writes the HTML, a PDF and a PowerPoint with one picture per click and
that click's notes to the git-ignored `_output/handover/`. Quarto's own pptx format was removed on
2026-10-06: it cannot use `custom.scss`, so its slides had none of the AIMS styling.

CI builds the figures (to show
the script runs), writes placeholder AIMS assets and local pictures, and fails if
`notes/speaker-notes.md` is stale.
It does not compare figure PNGs: the same code renders different bytes on Ubuntu and Windows,
and that check failed on every push from July to October 2026.

Figures are generated by a **standalone script**, not by executable chunks in the `.qmd`.
This is deliberate: it keeps slide rendering fast and dependency-free, so the deck can be
rebuilt on a machine that has Quarto but not `ssdtools`/`brms`. Regenerate figures only
when the underlying code changes.

### Packages in use

No `DESCRIPTION`. The canonical dependency list is the `library()` block at the top of
`R/figures.R`: `ggplot2`, `dplyr`, `tidyr`, `tibble`, `scales`, `patchwork`, `ssdtools`.
Ask before adding to it. The two local-only scripts also use `pdftools` and `magick`
(`R/external-figures.R`, and `R/aims-assets.R` when the source deck is present), called with
`::` and not added to that list.

## 3. Content rules

- **The science is the setting, not the subject.** This audience came for open source and
  tooling. Ecotoxicology detail earns its place only when it makes a software point.
- **Reuse concepts from the earlier decks, never the slides.** `notes/source-synthesis.md`
  records what may be reused and what must not be repeated. Read it before editing content.
- **The slides hold what is said.** The speaker does not want to read from notes (2026-10-06).
  Put what she would say on the slide as short text, built up with clicks, with the final point
  on its own click; the notes hold only the sentences that join the slides. More slides are
  acceptable, because she speaks quickly, but not for the science: on 2026-10-07 she judged a
  five-slide dredging build and a two-slide SSD build too long, and each was cut to one slide
  with its steps as clicks. Later that day she judged the animation overdone: keep builds to a
  few clicks, and where she prefers an earlier slide shown complete (the SSD), its narration
  stays in the notes. Where a picture changes with a click, stack
  versions drawn on identical canvases in a `.r-stack`.
- **She reviews in the PowerPoint handout** and edits the speaker notes there. Before
  re-running `R/export-deck.R`, which overwrites it, copy her file to `superceded/`, read each
  page's notes, and carry her edits into the `::: {.notes}` blocks. Each page's notes repeat
  the slide's earlier clicks and include the slide text in bold, so an edit can sit on any page
  of a build. The comments she sends number pages of the handout, one per click, not slides.
  A change she makes to the bold text is a change to the slide. Where she places it elsewhere
  in the narration, a `> Read the slide.` direction records the place (2026-10-09). Bold text
  she deletes is text she does not read out, not a deletion from the slide: on 2026-10-13 she
  deleted both columns of *Industry work and packages* from the notes while asking for a
  graphic on that slide. Mark such text `.unread`, which keeps it on the slide and out of the
  handout notes, and say which slides it was applied to, so she can correct it.
- **The narration is in the speaker's own register,** taken from her speaker notes in the
  earlier decks and described in `notes/source-synthesis.md` §"The speaker's register": first
  person, explanatory, no staged pauses, rhetorical questions or slogans. The parent
  `CLAUDE.md` §12 applies to slide text and notes alike.
- **Preferred terminology is listed in `notes/source-synthesis.md` §"Preferred
  terminology".** Match it exactly — particularly `"no effect"` in scare quotes, "guideline
  values", "fit-for-purpose", and the blood-alcohol analogy for a DGV.
- **Visual style:** sparse text, one idea per slide, build up in layers with reveal.js
  fragments, `→` as the connector, schematic redraws rather than screenshots.
- **Every factual claim must be traceable** to `notes/source-synthesis.md`, the CV, or
  the CSVs in `data/` (`git-evidence-*`, `bayesnec-*`). Do not invent download counts, dates, productivity multipliers
  or quotes.
- **Do not put invented numbers in a figure, even labelled "illustrative".** An early draft
  did exactly that for the productivity slide; it was plausible, directionally right and
  numerically wrong. It has been replaced by `R/git-evidence.R`, which measures the same
  thing from the commit history. If a claim is worth a figure, it is worth measuring — and
  if it genuinely cannot be measured, make it a sentence rather than a chart.

Some underlying work uses **confidential industry data** ("black literature" — the
speaker's own term). Nothing in this repo may contain raw industry data, client-identifying
monitoring results, or unpublished figures from commercial reports. Only use results that
are already in the public record via the listed publications.

**Treat the repo as public.** It was private on 2026-10-06 and is intended to be made public,
so everything committed here is held to that standard:

- `Relevant_publication_history_pdfs/` and `old_talks_and_background/` are `.gitignore`d —
  publisher copyright, third-party clip art, CV and bio. Never commit them.
- **Images by licence.** Generated figures are ours. The two WAMSI figures are CC BY 3.0 AU and

  licence that can be pointed at.
- Only results already in the public record via the listed publications.
- `prompts/` is public too. Keep the logs free of client names and unpublished results.

## 5. Things that will bite you

- `Rscript` and `quarto` are **not on PATH**. Use the absolute paths above.
- Sessions may run in WSL. Call the Windows R and Quarto by their `/mnt/c/Program Files/...`
  paths; PowerPoint (for `R/aims-assets.R`) and Edge are reachable the same way.
- `python3` is available in WSL; `pandoc` and command-line `poppler` are not. R's `pdftools`
  reads and renders PDFs on both sides.
- Quarto reserves the class `.aside` and places any block with it at the foot of the slide.
  Use `.minor` for small supporting text.
- Quarto's `auto-stretch` sizes a lone image to the remaining height whatever its width, which
  pushed wide figures off the slide. It is off; `img.fig-full` in `custom.scss` sizes figures.
  The class sits on the `<img>` itself, so `.fig-full img` matches nothing.
- reveal.js's print stylesheet (`?print-pdf`) sets section padding to zero with
  `html.reveal-print .reveal .slides section`. The padding rule in `custom.scss` must stay more
  specific than that, or the PDF export puts text over the blue strip.
- `magick::image_write()` keeps the source format unless `format =` is given, so a `.jpg` path
  can hold PNG data.
- Headless Edge, used for screenshots and the PDF export, printed blank pages when another
  headless instance was still running. Give it its own `--user-data-dir`.
- The deck sets `pdf-separate-fragments`, so the PDF has one page per click. `R/export-deck.R`
  counts each slide's clicks in the rendered HTML (fragments sharing a `data-fragment-index`
  count once) and stops unless the slide's notes have the same number of `> Click` lines.
- On a div, Quarto turns `fragment-index="1"` into `data-fragment-index`; on an image, write
  `data-fragment-index="1"` directly. Use them to make a picture appear with a line of text.
- `R/local-images.R` uses `Shape.Export()`, a hidden member of the PowerPoint object model. It
  keeps transparency; `Slide.Export()` does not. A COM session attaches to any PowerPoint the
  speaker already has open, so the script quits PowerPoint only if no other presentation is
  open.
- `on.exit()` at the top level of an `Rscript` file does nothing. Clean up explicitly, or inside
  a function.
- A fragment not yet shown still takes its space on the slide. A slide whose last click adds a
  figure must fit with everything shown, or the print splits it over two PDF pages and
  `R/export-deck.R` stops with a page count that does not match the clicks.
- An image laid over the whole slide (the red team slide) needs `.full-overlay`: reveal.js gives
  images a block margin, which shifted the overlay off the background picture beneath it.
- `R/aims-assets.R` quits PowerPoint after exporting the team slide without checking for other
  open presentations, unlike `R/local-images.R`. Close the handout before running it.
- The parent `CLAUDE.md` §7 requires backing up untracked files to `superceded/` before
  editing. This repo is now under git, so normal git status rules apply.
